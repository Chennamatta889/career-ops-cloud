Safety

How to spot suspicious job offers and recruitment scams

Practical checks before sharing documents, paying fees, or installing interview software.

By Career-Ops · Published 21 November 2026 · Practical guide

Verify the source

A professional-looking message is not proof of legitimacy. Search for the job on the employer's official careers website and compare the requisition number, title, and location. Check the sender's domain carefully: a lookalike spelling or free-mail address can be a warning sign.

Some legitimate agencies recruit on behalf of employers, so a different domain is not automatically fraudulent. Ask them to identify the client where permissible and verify the relationship through independent contact channels.

Watch for pressure and unusual requests

  • Requests to pay for an interview, security deposit, training kit, or guaranteed job placement.
  • Demands for passwords, one-time codes, bank login details, or remote-access software.
  • Promises of an offer without meaningful screening for a skilled role.
  • Pressure to act within minutes or move payment conversations away from official channels.
  • Attachments or links that require disabling your device's security settings.

Be especially careful with 'task' or 'optimization' jobs that require deposits or cryptocurrency transfers. Do not send money to unlock earnings.

Protect identity documents

Recruitment may involve identity and employment checks, but the timing and channel matter. Share sensitive records only after verifying the employer and understanding what is required. Use official portals, remove unnecessary data where permitted, and keep a record of what you provided.

Never share a one-time password or authentication recovery code with a recruiter. If an account is compromised, change credentials through the genuine service and review active sessions immediately.

What to do when something feels wrong

Stop engaging, save the original message and job link, and verify with the employer through a contact method obtained independently. Report fraudulent postings to the job platform and the appropriate cybercrime reporting authority. If you transferred money, contact your bank promptly.

A careful job search protects both your career and your personal information. A genuine opportunity will allow reasonable verification.

Put this into practice

Use this guide with a real job posting, verify all information, and keep your documents honest and secure.
