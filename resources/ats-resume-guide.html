Resumes

Write an ATS-friendly resume without losing your voice

A practical method for building a readable, evidence-based resume that fits real job requirements.

By Career-Ops · Published 21 November 2026 · Practical guide

Start with evidence, not a template

An applicant tracking system is often used to store and search applications, but each employer configures its process differently. There is no universal score that guarantees an interview. Focus first on a resume a recruiter can understand in 20 seconds: target role, relevant tools, recent experience, and evidence of impact.

Choose a straightforward one-column layout. Put contact information in normal document text, not exclusively in headers, footers, text boxes, or graphics. Use clear headings such as Summary, Experience, Projects, Skills, and Education. Export a selectable-text PDF unless the employer requests a different format.

Translate a posting into a skills map

Read the job posting once for responsibilities, a second time for required tools, and a third time for the outcomes the team is trying to deliver. Make a private two-column list: what the employer needs and what you can genuinely demonstrate. Include exact technology names where you have hands-on experience; do not add tools simply because a keyword scanner might prefer them.

  • Requirement: maintain CI/CD pipelines. Evidence: maintained Jenkins pipelines, resolved broken builds, and handled release stages.
  • Requirement: cloud troubleshooting. Evidence: investigated connectivity, identity permissions, and monitoring alerts in actual cloud environments.
  • Requirement: collaboration. Evidence: documented fixes, reviewed changes, and communicated incident status to teammates.

Write strong experience bullets

Use the structure action + scope + method + observable result. A credible bullet might read: 'Maintained Jenkins pipelines for multiple services; diagnosed dependency failures and documented fixes to reduce repeated build interruptions.' Add numbers only if you can explain how you measured them. If you cannot quantify a result, describe reliability, accuracy, or ownership instead.

Keep tense consistent. Use present tense for current responsibilities and past tense for completed projects. Include enough context for someone outside your company to understand what changed.

Tailor responsibly

Maintain one truthful master resume, then create a focused version for each role family. For a cloud operations role, move monitoring, infrastructure, incident response, and automation higher. For a developer role, prioritize programming, testing, APIs, and application outcomes. Do not rewrite job titles or invent ownership.

  1. Match the resume title to the actual role family.
  2. Move the three strongest relevant accomplishments into the opening half of page one.
  3. Check that every named tool can be discussed in an interview.
  4. Review dates, links, spelling, and filename before sending.

Final quality check

Open the PDF, select all text, and paste it into a plain text editor. If the text order is scrambled, simplify the formatting. Ask someone to identify your current role and strongest two skills after a quick scan. If they struggle, the layout is doing too much work.

An ATS-friendly resume is still a human communication document. Relevance, clarity, truthfulness, and proof beat keyword stuffing.

Put this into practice

Use this guide with a real job posting, verify all information, and keep your documents honest and secure.
