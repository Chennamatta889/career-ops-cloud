Communication

Write recruiter messages that are specific and respectful

Use a compact introduction, relevant evidence, and a clear next step when reaching out about jobs.

By Career-Ops · Published 21 November 2026 · Practical guide

Research before sending

Verify that the recruiter is connected to the employer or hiring team and that the opening is genuine. Use the company's career site or the recruiter's actual posting. Do not assume an email address from a guessed format or use private personal details collected without permission.

Your first message should reference a specific role, team, or business requirement. Generic mass messages rarely give someone a reason to respond.

Build a four-part message

  1. Context: say which role or post you are responding to.
  2. Fit: describe two truthful skills or outcomes relevant to that role.
  3. Ask: request the best application route or whether your experience is a match.
  4. Close: include a resume or profile link only where appropriate, and thank them.

Example: 'Hello, I saw the Cloud Engineer opening on your careers page. I have hands-on experience supporting CI/CD pipelines and cloud application operations, including incident troubleshooting. I have applied through the official site and would appreciate any guidance on next steps. Thank you.' Adapt it to facts you can prove.

Personalize without exaggerating

A single concrete sentence about a requirement is better than paragraphs of adjectives. Do not claim to be an immediate joiner if you have a notice period. Do not imply that a recruiter referred you or endorsed you unless that actually happened.

Keep attachments small and readable. If the role requires a portfolio, share a working project link rather than an unreviewed code dump.

Follow up with restraint

If you have a legitimate previous conversation or a posted deadline, one concise follow-up after a reasonable interval is usually enough. Respect an explicit refusal or opt-out immediately. Sending the same message repeatedly can damage your reputation and may violate platform policies.

Measure success by relevant conversations and interviews, not merely messages sent. A smaller list of genuinely suitable opportunities is more sustainable.

Put this into practice

Use this guide with a real job posting, verify all information, and keep your documents honest and secure.
