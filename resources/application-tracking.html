Job search

Build a simple job application tracking system

Keep an accurate record of jobs, follow-ups, interviews, and next actions without applying blindly.

By Career-Ops · Published 21 November 2026 · Practical guide

Track the information that changes decisions

You do not need an elaborate database. Record the role, company, official posting URL, location, date found, date applied, resume version, application stage, last interaction, and next action. Keep private notes about role fit and questions to raise at interview.

Separate 'saved' from 'applied'. Saving a job locally is not proof that a company received an application, and an automated job feed is not a confirmation that a vacancy is still open.

Use a manageable workflow

  1. Saved: interesting listing, not yet verified.
  2. Review: checked role fit and official employer link.
  3. Applied: application form or email actually submitted.
  4. Interview: a screening or interview has been confirmed.
  5. Offer or Closed: record the decision and close the loop.

Add an optional reminder for roles that deserve follow-up. A reminder should support a specific relationship or deadline, not generate repeated unsolicited messages.

Deduplicate by more than job title

The same vacancy may appear on multiple boards under slightly different titles. Use the employer, canonical job URL, requisition ID when present, and location to spot duplicates. When a listing is reposted, check whether it is a new requisition or the same job.

Keep a source URL so you can distinguish an old aggregator copy from the employer's current listing. Verify every role before applying.

Review outcomes weekly

Once a week, look for patterns: Are you applying to jobs that match your actual responsibilities? Are you receiving screening invitations? Is the resume version clear and role-specific? A quiet application pipeline can mean the target list needs refining, not that you should increase message volume.

Store copies of resumes securely. Avoid putting names, personal contact details, login credentials, or recruiter replies into a public repository or shared spreadsheet.

Put this into practice

Use this guide with a real job posting, verify all information, and keep your documents honest and secure.
